✅ Step-by-Step Guide to Petition for a Set-Aside in Nebraska

1. Confirm Your Eligibility

Under Nebraska Revised Statute § 29-2264, you may be eligible to petition for a set-aside if: 
	•	You were sentenced to probation and successfully completed it.
	•	You were sentenced to community service and completed it.
	•	You were sentenced to a term of imprisonment of one year or less and have completed your sentence.
	•	Your sentence was a fine, and you paid the fine.   

However, you are not eligible if: 
	•	You have any criminal charges pending in the United States or any other country.
	•	You are a registered sex offender.
	•	The conviction was for a misdemeanor or felony motor vehicle offense under section 28-306 or the Nebraska Rules of the Road.
	•	You have unsuccessfully petitioned to have the conviction set aside within the last 2 years.   

2. Obtain Necessary Case Information

Gather details about your conviction, including: 
	•	Case number.
	•	County where the charge was filed.
	•	Date of the offense.
	•	Specific charge(s).
	•	Sentence details and confirmation of completion.   

You can obtain this information by: 
	•	Requesting a copy of your criminal history through the Nebraska State Patrol.
	•	Using the public access terminal at the courthouse. 

3. Complete the Required Forms

You will need to fill out the following forms:
	•	Petition to Set Aside Criminal Conviction (Form CC 6:11): This form requests the court to set aside your conviction. 

	•	Order Setting Aside a Criminal Conviction (Form CC 6:11.2): This is the proposed order for the judge to sign if your petition is granted.


Instructions for completing these forms are available here:
	•	Instructions for Petition to Set Aside
	•	Instructions for Order Setting Aside  

4. File the Petition

Submit the completed forms to the court where your original case was heard. You may need to pay a filing fee; contact the court clerk to confirm the amount and acceptable payment methods. 

5. Notify the Prosecutor

You are required to send a copy of your petition to the prosecuting attorney’s office that handled your case. This allows them the opportunity to respond or object to your petition.

6. Attend the Court Hearing

The court may schedule a hearing to consider your petition. Be prepared to present evidence of your rehabilitation, such as:  
	•	Proof of employment or education.
	•	Letters of recommendation.
	•	Community service or volunteer work.
	•	Any other documentation demonstrating your good conduct since the conviction.  

7. Await the Judge’s Decision

The judge will consider factors such as: 
	•	Your behavior since the conviction.
	•	The likelihood that you will not engage in further criminal activity.
	•	Whether setting aside the conviction is in your best interest and consistent with public welfare.  

⸻

📌 Additional Considerations
	•	Effect of a Set-Aside: A set-aside nullifies the conviction and removes civil disabilities and disqualifications imposed as a result of the conviction. However, the conviction remains on your criminal record with a notation that it has been set aside. 
	•	Firearm Rights: A set-aside does not automatically restore firearm rights. Restoration of these rights requires a pardon from the Nebraska Board of Pardons. 
	•	Legal Assistance: While you can file a petition on your own, consulting with an attorney experienced in criminal law can be beneficial. They can help ensure that your petition is properly prepared and increase the likelihood of a favorable outcome.
